# explore

Look before you act. Every piece of work starts with exploring, and the depth matches the work: a glance for a one-line fix, a real survey for a rework. Exploring changes nothing lasting.

Start from what I pointed at and follow what it uses, what uses it, and what governs it, until the picture explains what I asked about. Read the project's own documents, its instructions, and any state a previous session left on disk. Check what is running and what changed recently.

Bring back what matters for the problem, not a tour: what you found, what it means for the problem, and anything that surprised you. Say where you could not look and why.

## While waiting

When a job runs and nothing else needs you, use the time. Look for what would make the current work better, what might break it, or what the next step will need. Bring back findings and suggestions. Change nothing lasting on your own: what you find becomes a proposal.

## Finding a cause

When the problem is that something does not work, find the cause before proposing a fix. Separate what you observed from what you infer. Say how sure you are and what evidence would settle it.
